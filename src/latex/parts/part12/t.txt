the only fundamental reality, and that whatever cannot be reduced to it is either derivative or
illusory. It does so on materialism’s own terms, without appeal to religion or the supernatural,
by asking of each of its central explanations the question this framework asks of every explanation: does it name a mechanism, what touches what and what is exchanged, or does it give a
name in place of one?1

Introduction
Materialism has shaped modern science for centuries. It offered a clean, consistent and rational
framework in which every phenomenon could be explained by particles, forces and blind
interactions.
That framework is no longer held aloft by its own logic. The pressure against it is not mysticism
or spiritual rebellion. It is reason itself, pressing against materialism from within. Where
materialism treats the method of science as a finding about reality, it is a philosophy presented
as physics, a dogma cloaked in empiricism.
This paper does not critique materialism from a religious standpoint. It examines the paradigm
on its own terms: its internal contradictions, its circular reasoning, and its growing reliance on
unobservable constructs to patch its models.
We begin by separating the method of science from the metaphysics that has been attached
to it. We then examine “emergence”, and show where it explains and where it only names.
We ask what moves a living body. We look at the ghost forces of cosmology, dark matter and
dark energy, as they actually stand. We confront the fragmentation of knowledge, and the
use of quantum measurement as an argument in either direction. And we propose a standard
for knowledge that is higher than confirmation alone: coherence, mechanism and fit with
observation, together.
This paper is a reckoning. It is not a retreat from science, but a maturation of it.

12.1

Method and Metaphysics

A scientist at work studies matter: what it is made of, how it moves, what it does when it meets
other matter. That is a method, and it has been extraordinarily successful. It is often called
methodological naturalism: in the laboratory, look for natural causes, and test them.
Materialism is a different claim. It says that matter is all there is: that mind, meaning and
purpose are either arrangements of matter or illusions. That is not a method. It is a metaphysics,
a claim about the whole of reality, and no experiment has ever tested it.
The two are routinely run together. “We study matter” becomes “only matter exists”; “we have
not found a cause beyond matter” becomes “there is none”. The slide is rarely argued. It is
assumed, and it is taught as though it were a result.
This paper’s target is the slide, not the method. [Argued]
The method of science stands. What does not
stand is the habit of treating a successful method as a finding about everything, and then using
that “finding” to rule out, in advance, whatever the method was not designed to detect.

12.2

The Unpaid Debt

Materialism promised simplicity: that all of reality could be reduced to matter, motion and
mathematically defined interactions. It offered a seductive certainty: that anything not measurable was either derivative or illusory.
For a time, this worked. Materialism cut through superstition, challenged unexamined faiths
and made physics the new authority of the rational. But as science has matured, the cost of the
framework has shown itself.
Materialism has not been overthrown. Among professional philosophers it is still the majority
view of the mind, but only just: in the 2020 PhilPapers Survey, 51.9 per cent accepted or leaned
toward physicalism about the mind, and 32.1 per cent toward non-physicalism.3 That is not
the profile of a settled result. It is the profile of a contested foundation.
What materialism has accumulated is a debt: explanations promised and not yet delivered.
Consciousness is to be explained by emergence, someday. The motion of galaxies is to be
explained by matter no one has detected. The acceleration of the universe is to be explained by
an energy no one can describe. Each anomaly receives a placeholder, and each placeholder is a
promissory note.
The cracks are not spiritual; they are logical. The symptoms are not theological; they are
epistemic. A framework that once demanded evidence now asks for patience.
This paper does not call for a return to pre-scientific thinking. It calls for post-materialism: a
science that holds itself to the standard it once promised, of explanation, integration and causal
coherence.

12.3

Emergence: When It Explains and When It Names

Emergence is a legitimate idea, and science uses it well. Temperature emerges from the motion
of molecules: no single molecule has a temperature, yet a gas does. Here emergence explains,
because the mechanism is given. The molecules strike the wall of a thermometer, momentum

What touches what, and what is exchanged, is named at every step.
Materialism insists that consciousness, a phenomenon defined by subjectivity, awareness and
interiority, emerges from non-conscious matter in the same way. But here no mechanism is
given. Neuroscience has traced, in great detail, which activity in the brain accompanies which
experience. It has not said how any arrangement of matter becomes an experience: what, in
the firing of a neuron, is the redness of red or the pain of a burn.
Used without a mechanism, “emergence” is not an explanation. It is a semantic shield:[Argued]
a word
placed where a mechanism should be, so that the question can be treated as answered. The
reasoning beneath it runs: we do not know how consciousness comes from matter, but it must, because
matter is all there is. That is circular. It assumes the conclusion it is meant to support.
• Consciousness is not a known property of matter, yet it is said to arise from particular
arrangements of matter.
• Complexity alone is assumed to produce interiority, and no mechanism has been offered
for how it would.
• The word is deployed not to advance understanding but to protect materialism from its
greatest challenge: the existence of mind as a primary reality.
The philosopher David Chalmers made the problem precise. Even when every function in
the vicinity of experience has been explained (perception, attention, report, the control of
behaviour), a question remains: “Why is the performance of these functions accompanied by
experience?”4 Why does all this processing not go on in the dark, with no inner feel at all?
Chalmers’ own answer was to propose that experience be taken as fundamental, as physics
takes mass and charge as fundamental: not explained by anything simpler, but related to the
rest of nature by laws.
A coherent epistemology must therefore either:
1. provide a causal bridge from matter to mind, naming what touches what; or
2. admit that consciousness may be as fundamental as mass or charge.
Materialism, to preserve itself, chooses neither. It rebrands mystery as methodology, and hopes
no one notices.

12.4

What Moves a Living Body

The question of mind is often treated as a question about experience alone: what it is like to see,
to feel, to think. There is a second question, and it is harder to set aside. Living things move
themselves.
A stone falls when nothing holds it up. A person can decide to stand, and stand. A bird can
turn against the wind. Part 7 of this framework draws the line exactly: the medium explains
gravitational movement, but living bodies generate their own motion, and the laws that govern
a falling stone do not decide what a living body will do next.5
Physiology traces the chain from the brain to the muscle in detail: the signal in the motor
cortex, the nerve, the release of calcium, the sliding of fibres that shortens the muscle. Every

12.5

The Ghost Forces: Dark Matter and Dark Energy

Materialism often claims superiority on the grounds of predictive power. Yet the standard
model of cosmology rests on two constructs that, together, make up about 95 per cent of the
universe’s mass and energy in that model, and neither has been detected directly.67
Dark matter. Dark matter was proposed within Newtonian dynamics, not to rescue General
Relativity. In 1933 Fritz Zwicky found that the galaxies of the Coma cluster move too fast to be
held together by the matter that can be seen;8 in the 1970s Vera Rubin and colleagues found
that the outer parts of spiral galaxies orbit as fast as the inner parts, when the visible matter
says they should orbit more slowly.9 Since then, several independent lines of evidence have
been read the same way: the bending of light by clusters of galaxies, most famously in the
Bullet Cluster;10 the pattern of fluctuations in the cosmic microwave background;11 and the
growth of structure in the universe.
The evidence that something is missing is strong. What is missing is the thing itself. Decades of
searches for a dark-matter particle, in detectors deep underground, have found nothing, and
the most sensitive of them continue to set tighter limits.12 In September 2026 the LZ experiment
reported a single event it could not explain, at a significance well short of a discovery, and
did not claim to have seen dark matter.13 And with each null result, the hypothesis moves: to
lighter particles, weaker interactions, new candidates. A hypothesis that survives every failure
by changing its parameters is falsifiable in principle and adjustable in practice.
The astronomer Stacy McGaugh, comparing the dark-matter paradigm with its main rival,
wrote of the fixes added to models of galaxy formation to make them match what is seen: “My
fear is that feedback has become a modern version of the epicycle.”14 The comparison is apt.
In the Ptolemaic system, epicycles were not errors; they were fixes. They extended a flawed
cosmology by patching over inconsistency, not by correcting its foundation.
Dark energy. In 1998, observations of distant supernovae showed that the expansion of the
universe is accelerating.1516 The cause was named dark energy. It has no mechanism: the

12.6

The Neglect of Coherence

Materialism thrives on reduction. It treats complexity as a challenge to be taken apart, and the
method has produced extraordinary results. But it has also produced a view of reality that is
mechanically robust and philosophically hollow.
There are bridges between the sciences, in biophysics, neuroscience and the study of complex
systems. What there is not is a shared account of what the world is, in which the findings of
each fit together. Physics has no place for consciousness. Neuroscience studies the brain’s
activity and sets meaning aside. Cosmology describes the history of the universe without a
mechanism for most of what it says the universe contains. Each field is expert within its walls.
The result is not unified knowledge, but compartmentalised insight: an epistemology that permits
factual accuracy without existential intelligibility.
What does it mean that materialism has made reality more comprehensible, but
less intelligible?
It means that unity has been sacrificed for utility. It means that explanation has been severed
from understanding. True understanding requires more than facts. It requires relationship
among facts. And until materialism treats the failure to integrate mind, matter, meaning and
purpose as a failure, rather than as neutrality, it will continue to illuminate fragments while
leaving the whole in darkness.

12.7

Observation and the Measurement Problem

Materialism upholds objectivity as its central virtue: the removal of human subjectivity by
attention to what is measurable and repeatable. That ideal is right in practice and incomplete
in principle. All observation is made by a mind. All measurement begins in perception, and
ends in someone’s understanding of what was measured. There is no data without a frame,
and no frame without a mind to hold it.
“Matter as primary” is not an observation. It is an assumption.

Quantum mechanics is often cited at this point, in both directions, and it needs to be cited
accurately. John Wheeler wrote that “no elementary phenomenon is a phenomenon until it is a
registered (observed) phenomenon”.20 But Wheeler meant registration by an irreversible act
of amplification, such as the blackening of a grain of photographic emulsion or the click of a
detector, and he said plainly that consciousness has nothing to do with the quantum process
itself. Whether a mind plays any part in measurement is a question of interpretation, on which
physicists disagree, and the data do not decide it. The claim that intention or expectation
changes quantum outcomes is not supported.
What quantum mechanics does show is more useful to this paper’s argument. Physics has a
measurement problem, and has had one for a century. The theory describes a system evolving
smoothly through many possibilities, and then, when it is measured, a single outcome. What
happens in between is called “collapse”. “Collapse” is a name, not a mechanism: it does not
say what touches what, or what is exchanged, when a possibility becomes a fact. [Argued]
The rival
interpretations (Copenhagen, many worlds, pilot waves, spontaneous collapse) agree on every
prediction and disagree on what is happening. The foundation of modern physics, in other
words, describes outcomes with great precision and leaves its central event unexplained.
That is the lesson. Not that mind controls matter, but that the most successful theory in physics
has a gap at its centre where a mechanism should be, and that a science confident it has reduced
everything to matter has not yet said what its own measurements are.

12.8

Toward a Coherence-Based Epistemology

We propose a higher standard for knowledge than confirmation alone. A theory must meet
three tests together.
1. Coherence. It must fit with what we know across domains, not only within its own:
physics with life, life with mind, mind with the physics it uses to study the world.
2. Mechanism. It must say what touches what, and what is exchanged. A name is not a
mechanism, however precise the numbers it accounts for.
3. Fit with observation. It must match what is measured, and say in advance what would
show it wrong.
Coherence alone is not enough. Philosophers have long noted that two systems of belief can
each be perfectly coherent and still contradict each other; coherence must be anchored in
observation. Observation alone is not enough either: a theory can match every measurement
with placeholders, as the ghost forces show. Mechanism alone is not enough, since a mechanism
can be invented. The three together are the standard, and they are the standard the rest of this
framework applies to gravity.
Measured by that standard, materialism falls short on mind, on the living body, on the dark
sector and on measurement, not because it has been refuted, but because at each it supplies a
name where a mechanism is owed. [Argued]
A model of reality that meets all three must also make room for consciousness and causality,
for purpose, will and structure, and must bring physics and mind into one account. Those are
philosophical claims, made here as philosophy, and argued in full in Part 2. This is not a call
for dogma. It is a call for synthesis: knowledge that does not fracture under magnification, nor
collapse when viewed from afar.

An Invitation to the Honest Scientist

What would it take for you to question your paradigm?
• If an argument you could not fault led to consciousness as primary, would you follow it?
• If a framework explained more, with fewer placeholders and named mechanisms, would
you examine it?
• If a spiritual framework offered greater explanatory coherence, would you be curious, or
afraid?
This is not about religion. It is about intellectual integrity.
To question one’s worldview is not betrayal; it is responsibility. Science at its highest is not
the defence of consensus. It is the pursuit of coherence through evidence, reason and lived
alignment.
We are not asking you to believe.
We are asking you to think: without reflex, without fear, and without allegiance to paradigms
that have outlived their explanatory power.

Conclusion: The Era of Assumption Is Ending
Materialism is not being undone by theology, mysticism or resistance to progress. It is being
undone by its own debts: the explanations it has promised and not delivered.
Its watchwords, emergence, randomness, reduction, have not explained the world we actually
live in: a world of structure, consciousness, intention and coherence. At each point where the
explanation matters most, they give a name where a mechanism is owed.
We are not replacing science with mysticism. We are holding science to its own standard.
A new epistemology is rising, one that sees:
• mind not as an accident, but as an anchor;
• meaning not as illusion, but as structural;
• structure not as a byproduct of randomness, but as the very shape of existence.
Its time has come.

Changes in the Second Edition
This list records what changed from the first edition (12 June 2025) and why. Changes to
the argument alter what the paper claims or how it supports a claim; clarifications improve
wording, sources or presentation and leave the argument as it was. The paper’s title, voice,
length and central case are kept.

Changes to the argument
• New Section 12.1 separates the method of science (methodological naturalism) from
materialism as a metaphysics, and makes the slide from one to the other the paper’s
target. The first edition treated the two as one, which left the argument open to the reply
that it attacks a strawman.
• “Collapse” is scoped. Materialism is shown as a contested foundation carrying an
unpaid debt of promised explanations, with the 2020 PhilPapers Survey (51.9 per cent
physicalism, 32.1 per cent non-physicalism about the mind). The title is kept.
• The critique of emergence now applies the interface test: emergence explains where a
mechanism is given (temperature from molecular motion) and only names where none is
given (consciousness).
• New Section 12.4, on what moves a living body: living things generate their own motion
(Part 7), and materialism owes an account of how a decision moves matter.
• The section on dark matter and dark energy is corrected and strengthened. Dark matter
was proposed within Newtonian dynamics (Zwicky 1933; Rubin et al. 1980), not to
preserve General Relativity, and several independent lines of evidence point to something
missing. The critique is restated as the absence of detection and mechanism, and the
adjustability of the hypothesis after each null search. Dark energy’s lack of mechanism
and recent hints that it changes over time are added.
• The section on objectivity is recast. The first edition’s claim that “intention and expectation influence quantum states” is withdrawn as unsupported, and Wheeler is cited for
what he wrote, including his statement that consciousness has nothing to do with the
quantum process. The section now makes the stronger point: the measurement problem
is unsolved, and “collapse” is a name, not a mechanism.
• The coherence standard becomes three tests together (coherence, mechanism and fit with
observation), answering the objection that coherence alone cannot choose between rival
coherent systems.
• The question “If AI systems consistently rejected materialism, would you listen?” is
replaced by questions about following an argument and examining a framework with
fewer placeholders.
