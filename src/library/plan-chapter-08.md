They represent a claim on capability generated, not on resources extracted. Every CC therefore carries intrinsic worth measurable through publicly available metrics—education attainment, health improvement, carbon reduction, and verified service hours.

Redefining Value Global data indicate that approximately 42–50 % of GDP in both highand middleincome countries originates in services related to health, education, and public administration (World Bank, 2023). Yet less than 15 % of this contribution is compensated through direct public transfers. Civic Credits close that gap by transforming under-compensated social productivity into a formal economic instrument.

Let the verified civic output Qc be measured in standardized service hours, each hour indexed to the median hourly GDP contribution gm of the nation. Then the base Civic Credit value is defined as VCC = gm × hv , where hv is the verified service hours recorded through LifeLink and authenticated by PECs. This ensures every CC corresponds to an equivalent share of national productivity, empirically grounded in GDP data.

### 8.2 Global Integration and the Civic Credit Exchange (CCX)

Purpose This section defines the mechanisms through which Civic Credits (CC) operate across borders. Design Philosophy Global economic integration must not replicate the asymmetries of prior monetary systems. CCX is therefore designed around three pillars:

1\. Reciprocity: every exchange reflects equal civic value, not political or financial dominance.

2\. Transparency: all trades are publicly auditable on a permissioned blockchain managed by the International Civic Credit Registry (ICCR).

3\. Ethical Neutrality: no single nation or corporate entity can issue or manipulate global Civic Credit supply.

Exchange Architecture Structure The CCX operates as a multilayered system composed of:

- National Civic Treasuries (NCTs) — issue and redeem CC at domestic level;
- Regional Clearing Hubs (RCHs) — aggregate and net regional transactions;
- Global Civic Credit Exchange (CCX Core) — final settlement layer operated by the ICCR.

Each layer uses standardized smart-contract protocols to reconcile balances in near real time. Settlement occurs in Civic Value Units (CVU), the harmonized cross-border denomination equivalent to the weighted global median of verified service value.

If a national Civic Credit drifts outside that range for more than 30 days, the CCSF automatically intervenes by adjusting issuance or redemption rates until parity is restored.

Transaction Flow Cross-border CC transactions follow a five-step automated path:

1\. Initiation: A verified LifeLink wallet requests a cross-border payment.

2\. Validation: The sending NCT confirms proof-of-activity origin (PEC ledger reference).

3\. Conversion: CC amount converted to CVU at current parity rate via CCX smart contract.

4\. Settlement: Receiving NCT credits equivalent domestic CC to recipient account.

5\. Audit: Transaction hash stored in ICCR public ledger for transparency.

The CCX ensures compliance with international capital-flow regulations by restricting use of Civic Credits to verified social, educational, or ecological trade—explicitly excluding speculative finance, weapons, or extractive commodities.

8.7 Global Governance and Ethical Oversight Architecture Purpose The permanence of the People of the World framework depends not only on technical sustainability but on lawful and ethical governance. This section defines the institutional structure that guarantees transparency, legitimacy, and human rights protection at planetary scale. It formalizes how the International Civic Credit Registry (ICCR), the Poverty Elimination Center Authority (PECA), and the LifeLink Ethics and Integrity Board (LEIB) interact as autonomous but interdependent organs within a unified global governance architecture.

Structural Principles Governance within the Civic Economy is built on six inviolable principles:

1\. Subsidiarity: decisions are made at the lowest competent level—local PECs handle local affairs, global bodies arbitrate cross-border issues.

2\. Transparency: all actions, audits, and budgets are published in real time through open ledgers.

3\. Ethical Primacy: no efficiency or profit objective may override dignity, consent, or ecological integrity.

4\. Polycentric Accountability: power is distributed horizontally among institutions, preventing monopolization.

5\. Scientific Verifiability: all policy claims must be supported by public, reproducible data.

6\. Constitutional Permanence: core civic instruments are entrenched in international law and national constitutions.

Institutional Structure The global Civic Governance System consists of five principal bodies operating under treaty authority.

1\. International Civic Credit Registry (ICCR)

- Serves as the planetary ledger of trust, maintaining global standards for Civic Credits, LifeLink data formats, and interoperability.
- Governed by a Council of Member States, each with one vote, weighted by population and verified ethical compliance.
- Manages the Global Continuity Index (GCI) and certifies national readiness and performance.
- Financed through a fixed micro-levy (0.03%) on cross-border Civic Credit settlements.

2\. Poverty Elimination Center Authority (PECA)

- Coordinates PEC design standards, service benchmarks, and workforce training globally.
- Oversees the Global PEC Registry (GPR)—a real-time database of all PEC locations, outputs, and resilience scores.
- Issues Operational Compliance Certificates (OCCs) to national PEC systems every three years.
- Reports jointly to ICCR and national governments; decisions require dual ratification to prevent unilateral control.

3\. LifeLink Ethics and Integrity Board (LEIB)

- Ensures algorithmic fairness, data privacy, and human rights compliance across all digital systems.
- Composed of 36 members: one-third appointed by nations, one-third by academic and civil society institutions, and one-third by international ethics organizations.
- Maintains the Ethical Compliance Ledger (ECL)—a blockchain-based repository of all algorithmic audit results.
- Holds quasi-judicial power to issue binding injunctions suspending unethical system components until remediation.

4\. Civic Arbitration Tribunal (CAT)

- Serves as the global court for Civic Credit disputes, data rights violations, and intergovernmental grievances.
- Jurisdiction derived from the Global Civic Charter (GCC), recognized as an annex to the UN Digital Rights Convention.
- Operates under public proceedings, with mandatory publication of all rulings within 30 days.
- Appeals limited to questions of law and handled by the International Civic Court (ICCt).

5\. Global Secretariat for Capability and Ecology (GSCE)

- Integrates socio-economic and ecological governance.
- Maintains the Planetary Resilience Index (PRI) introduced in §5.14, auditing environmental equilibrium, energy access, and carbon neutrality.
- Coordinates cross-institutional research with UNEP, FAO, and the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).
