# Civic Credits, Simply

*An everyday guide for citizens and small businesses*

By Shareef Ali Rashada

#### The Short Story

Civic Credits are a way to pay people for essential work without borrowing money or raising prices. When someone finishes a day of work in healthcare, education, housing, or other community services, Civic Credits appear in their wallet the next morning. They can spend them like cash because shops can accept them, governments take them for taxes and fees, and businesses can swap them for dollars if they want.

#### What They Are (in plain language)

Think of Civic Credits like a gift card that works everywhere in town—and the balance is loaded only after real work happens. The credits are created by a public clearinghouse when your shift is verified. No debt is taken on. No IOUs. Just recognition of work already done.

#### How It Works for a Worker

Meet Maya, a nurse. She signs a simple add-on to her job contract saying she’ll be paid in Civic Credits. She clocks in and does her normal shift. Overnight, her wage in Civic Credits shows up in her phone wallet. The next day she can buy groceries, pay her bus fare, and cover part of her utilities with the same tap-to-pay she already uses.

#### How It Works for a Shop or Business

A grocery store turns on Civic Credit acceptance in the same payment terminal they already use. When Maya pays, the store can keep those credits to pay local taxes or suppliers who also accept them, or they can swap the credits for dollars at the clearinghouse at the same value. It’s like taking a card payment: settled quickly, no hassle.

#### Why Civic Credits Hold Their Value

They hold value because they are tied to real work. Every new credit is created only after someone has finished a shift teaching, caring, building, or repairing. The government also accepts them for taxes and licenses, so businesses always have somewhere to spend them. And any shop can exchange them for dollars at a stable rate during the rollout.

#### Will Prices Go Up?

General prices don’t need to rise, because credits aren’t printed “just because.” They are only created when new services are delivered. More nurses working means shorter wait times. More builders working means fewer backlogs. Supply rises with demand—that’s the point.

#### What Changes for You

For workers, pay comes in the morning after your shift, same as always, just in Civic Credits. For shops, you toggle on another payment type and can swap to dollars whenever you choose. For families, the things you need—appointments, repairs, care—become easier to get because positions are finally filled.

#### Common Questions, Straight Answers

Is this a second kind of money? It’s a public settlement token used alongside dollars. In the beginning it’s always worth the same as a dollar because shops can swap it at the clearinghouse and governments accept it for taxes.

Can I still be paid in dollars? Yes. Civic Credits are voluntary. They start where staff shortages are worst so communities see the benefit quickly.

What about taxes on my wages? Your Civic Credit income is taxed just like regular income. The tax office accepts the credits, which makes paying taxes easier.

What if my local shop doesn’t accept Civic Credits yet? Many will from day one, especially big chains and essential services. You can also use your credits to pay fees, transport, and utilities. Acceptance grows as people see it working.

How do we prevent cheating? Credits are created only after shifts are verified. Trying to create credits without real work is fraud and is punished. Quality checks make sure the work is done properly. Could this hurt the regular economy? It helps it. Civic Credits fill roles that have gone empty because budgets are tight. Shops get more paying customers. Governments get essential services delivered without new debt.

Can I change my mind later? Yes. You can switch back to dollar wages if you want. Employers can also choose to keep paying in dollars. Civic Credits are an option, not a mandate.

#### A Day in the Life

Maya’s 8-hour shift posts 320 Civic Credits to her wallet (40 credits per hour). She uses 50 for groceries, 10 for a bus pass, and 30 for a utility bill. The grocer keeps 20 credits to pay local fees and swaps the other 30 for dollars that same afternoon. Everyone wins: Maya got paid, the grocer got cash, the city collected fees, and the hospital kept its nurse.

#### Why This Ends “Money Says No”

We already have the people and the skills. What we run out of is the budget line. Civic Credits fix that. When the work gets done, the ability to pay appears. No debt. No waiting. Just the services a community needs, funded by the work itself.

#### Getting Started

If you’re a worker, ask your employer about joining. If you’re a shop, talk to your payment provider about turning on Civic Credits at your terminal. If you’re a local official, enable Civic Credits for taxes and fees. Once those three pieces are in place, the system runs on everyday life.

#### The Promise

Civic Credits are a simple promise kept: when you do the work that keeps a community alive, the community can always pay you. That’s how we make poverty unnecessary and dignity normal.
