Why Graviton Pressure Theory Is Not “Alternative Gravity”

A constraints-first explanation for educated readers

By Shareef Ali Rashada · 961 words · about 4 min · 2026 · Plain text

The phrase “alternative gravity” sounds innocuous, even fair-minded. It suggests a marketplace of competing ideas, each offering a different story about the same phenomenon. But this framing quietly misrepresents what Graviton Pressure Theory (GPT) is trying to do—and, more importantly, what problem it is responding to.

GPT does not begin by proposing a new mechanism and asking to be compared against existing ones. It begins by asking a prior, often skipped question:

What must any complete theory of gravity be able to explain?

That question changes everything. It shifts gravity from being treated as a mathematical tool optimized for certain regimes, to being treated as a physical phenomenon that continuously acts on the world we inhabit.

The usual framing—and its hidden assumption

In contemporary physics, General Relativity (GR) is treated as the foundational theory of gravity. Other ideas—modified gravity, emergent gravity, pressure models—are typically labeled alternatives. The implicit assumption is that GR already defines what “gravity” fundamentally is, and any challenger must justify deviating from that definition.

But this assumes the conclusion. It presumes that GR’s chosen ontology—gravity as spacetime geometry rather than force or interaction—is not just successful, but sufficient.

GPT rejects that presumption, not by attacking GR’s successes, but by questioning whether the domain of explanation has been prematurely narrowed.

Gravity as it is actually encountered

Long before gravity is inferred from galaxies or encoded in field equations, it is encountered locally and continuously:

• weight
• resistance when standing
• fatigue and structural load
• sedimentation and stratification
• the long-term ordering of matter
• the persistent sense of “down”

These are not marginal effects. They are gravity’s dominant interface with human life, engineering, and the material world. They are also the phenomena that any theory must explain before it is allowed to extrapolate to cosmology.

GPT begins here—not because these effects are more “intuitive,” but because they are more constraining. They occur everywhere, all the time, under conditions that can be directly interrogated.

What GR does—and does not—claim

General Relativity excels at predicting motion: orbits, lensing, timing, wave propagation. In these domains, it is extraordinarily successful.

However, GR’s own interpretation explicitly removes gravity from the category of force or interaction. In GR’s ontology, freely falling objects are not acted upon at all; they are inertial. What we call “gravity” is encoded in geometry, not in an active physical agent.

This choice has consequences.

It means that many everyday gravitational phenomena—standing, holding, loading, fatigue—are not treated as gravity doing something. They are reclassified as non-gravitational forces acting in a curved spacetime. Gravity becomes what happens when nothing resists it.

GPT does not say this is mathematically wrong. It says it is ontologically incomplete.

The role of constraints

The Gravity Catalog was developed precisely to avoid arguing at the level of preference or interpretation. It sets out constraints—observable facts that any theory of gravity must be able to account for without evasion.

Examples include:

• gravity has persistent directionality
• gravity acts continuously, even in equilibrium
• resisting gravity incurs real energetic cost
• gravity produces long-term structural ordering
• explanations must compose globally, not only locally
• foundational explanations must be literal, not metaphor-dependent

These are not GPT assumptions. They are properties of gravity as experienced and engineered.

Once these constraints are accepted, a striking asymmetry appears.

GR can describe many of these phenomena indirectly or by reclassification, but it does not own them as gravitational effects. It explains motion exceptionally well, but remains largely silent on gravity as continuous interaction.

GPT was constructed specifically to address that silence.

Why this is not “alternative gravity”

Calling GPT an alternative gravity theory implies that it merely swaps one explanatory story for another within the same conceptual game.

That is not what is happening.

GPT does not begin by rejecting GR. It begins by refusing to let gravity be defined only by the subset of phenomena GR handles most cleanly. It treats local, continuous gravity as foundational rather than derivative.

In this sense, GPT is not an alternative theory of gravity—it is a constraint-driven reconstruction of what gravity must be if all observed phenomena are taken seriously.

If a geometric theory could meet those constraints without evasion, GPT would have no reason to exist.

Dark matter and the cost of ignoring constraints

The widespread acceptance of dark matter and dark energy illustrates the cost of neglecting local constraints.

When gravity is defined primarily through large-scale motion and geometry, discrepancies at galactic and cosmological scales are naturally addressed by adding unseen content. This preserves the geometric framework, but it does so by increasing theoretical load rather than revisiting gravity’s ontology.

From a constraints-first perspective, this is not obviously the most conservative move. It is simply the most convenient one, given the chosen starting point.

GPT does not claim that dark matter is disproven. It claims that the need for it may be a symptom of having defined gravity too narrowly, too early.

What GPT is actually proposing

GPT proposes that gravity is a real, continuous physical interaction arising from pressure gradients in a graviton field. Whether this proposal is ultimately correct is an empirical question.

What matters here is why it was proposed at all.

GPT exists because a theory of gravity that cannot explain weight, resistance, and equilibrium as gravitational phenomena is incomplete—no matter how successful it is at explaining distant motion.

The proper order of explanation

A complete theory of gravity must earn the right to explain the universe by first explaining the ground beneath our feet.

This is the logic GPT follows.

Not rebellion.
Not contrarianism.
Not preference.

Constraint.

That is why GPT is not “alternative gravity.”
It is gravity, rebuilt from the bottom up, under the demand that nothing real be explained away.
