The Gravity Constraint Set

A foundational constraint set for any complete theory of gravity

By Shareef Ali Rashada · 925 words · about 4 min · 2026 · Plain text

Status of This Document

This document is not a theory of gravity.
It proposes no mechanism, no equations, and no preferred ontology.

Its purpose is strictly methodological.

It records what gravity demonstrably does, across scales and contexts, and states the minimum explanatory obligations any theory must meet to be considered complete rather than merely predictive.

No phenomenon listed here may be excluded, deferred, reclassified, or dismissed as “engineering,” “approximation,” or “outside scope” without explicit justification.

If a theory cannot address an item without evasion, that limitation is a property of the theory—not of the phenomenon.

Constraint 1: Persistent Directionality

Observation

Gravity establishes a stable, continuous directional bias in physical systems.

On Earth, this direction is locally invariant: objects fall, fluids stratify, structures load, and biological systems orient consistently with respect to a downward direction. This persists in static, dynamic, and equilibrium conditions.

Requirement

A complete theory of gravity must explain:

  • why a directional preference exists at all
  • why it is persistent rather than transient
  • why it is locally coherent across an extended region
  • why the direction aligns consistently for all material systems

This explanation must be literal, not metaphorical.

Failure Mode

Appealing only to trajectories, coordinate choices, or local analogies without providing a physically coherent account of directionality as an ongoing condition.

Constraint 2: Resistance as a Primary Manifestation

Observation

Gravity is most commonly encountered not as motion, but as resistance.

Standing, bracing, holding, stacking, and supporting all require continuous opposition to gravity. The dominant human and structural interaction with gravity is static load, not free fall.

Requirement

A complete theory must account for:

  • why resisting gravity requires effort
  • why resistance is continuous even in equilibrium
  • why load can be transferred, distributed, and concentrated

This resistance must arise from gravity itself, not be explained away as external or incidental.

Failure Mode

Treating resistance as non-gravitational while reserving gravity only for idealized free motion.

Constraint 3: Continuous Interaction in Equilibrium

Observation

Gravitational effects persist even when nothing moves.

A book resting on a table, a column supporting a roof, or a person standing upright are not free of gravity. Structural stress, compression, and fatigue accumulate over time despite static conditions.

Requirement

A complete theory must explain:

  • what gravity is doing during equilibrium
  • how interaction persists without motion
  • why equilibrium under gravity is not neutral

Failure Mode

Asserting that gravity is “absent” or “inactive” in equilibrium states.

Constraint 4: Maintenance Cost Against Gravity

Observation

Maintaining a position against gravity incurs real energetic cost.

Muscles fatigue, materials creep, and active systems consume energy to oppose gravitational effects. This cost is directional, persistent, and cumulative.

Requirement

A complete theory must account for:

  • why energy is required to maintain position
  • where that energy goes
  • how gravity couples to fatigue, wear, and dissipation

Failure Mode

Offloading energy accounting to unrelated domains without explaining why gravity necessitates it.

Constraint 5: Structural Ordering Over Time

Observation

Gravity produces long-term ordering effects.

Sedimentation, stratification, sagging, settling, and creep occur gradually under constant conditions. These effects encode gravity into structure over time.

Requirement

A complete theory must explain:

  • why gravity produces irreversible ordering
  • why time under gravity matters
  • why structure records gravitational history

Failure Mode

Treating long-term effects as incidental material behavior disconnected from gravity.

Constraint 6: Structure-Sensitive Response

Observation

While bulk free-fall appears universal, gravitational interaction during contact, resistance, and support is sensitive to internal structure, geometry, and material composition.

Different structures respond differently to identical gravitational conditions.

Requirement

A complete theory must allow for:

  • structure-dependent interaction without violating universality of free fall
  • differential response under load and support
  • coupling between gravity and internal organization

Failure Mode

Restricting gravity to idealized test-mass behavior only.

Constraint 7: Global Coherence of Local Experience

Observation

Gravity behaves coherently across extended regions.

People can stand on opposite sides of a planet, structures load consistently everywhere, and directionality remains globally stable without contradiction.

Requirement

A complete theory must provide:

  • a globally coherent account of gravity
  • compatibility between local explanations and planetary geometry
  • freedom from internally contradictory analogies

Failure Mode

Relying on explanations that work only locally but fail when globally composed.

Constraint 8: Independence from Pedagogical Metaphor

Observation

Explanations of gravity frequently rely on metaphors that are acknowledged to be false when taken literally.

These metaphors are nonetheless used to substitute for physical explanation.

Requirement

A complete theory must be expressible:

  • without recourse to knowingly false analogies
  • without requiring reinterpretation of literal statements
  • without shifting explanatory burden onto the listener

Failure Mode

Defending contradictions by appealing to “the spirit” rather than the literal content of explanations.

Constraint 9: Continuity Across Scales

Observation

Gravity operates seamlessly from human to planetary to galactic scales.

The same phenomenon governs posture, architecture, orbits, and cosmic structure.

Requirement

A complete theory must offer:

  • causal continuity across scales
  • consistency of explanation, even if mathematical form changes
  • no ontological switching between domains

Failure Mode

Invoking fundamentally different explanations at different scales without causal linkage.

Constraint 10: Minimal Assumptive Load

Observation

Local gravitational phenomena are direct, repeatable, and assumption-light.

They do not depend on ancient signals, extreme inference chains, or cosmological priors.

Requirement

A complete theory should be constrained first by:

  • local, continuous phenomena
  • high-frequency, repeatable interactions
  • minimal auxiliary assumptions

Failure Mode

Granting epistemic priority to indirect or assumption-heavy data while neglecting ubiquitous local effects.

Closing Note

This catalog does not privilege any theory.

It establishes what must be explained before explanation can be considered complete.

Any theory—existing or proposed—that cannot meet these constraints without evasion remains, by definition, incomplete.

No conclusion is drawn here.

The burden now lies with explanation.
