The search for meaning

You've felt it too.

Why am I here? Is there more than this? And what is that quiet voice that knows right from wrong, even when no one is watching? Almost everyone asks these questions. Here is a place to ask them honestly, and some answers worth considering.

Why we search

The search itself is the clue.

Of all the creatures on Earth, only we can grasp ideas like justice, mercy and truth. We don't just survive; we long to be good, to be fair, to do right by people we will never meet.

Survival alone doesn't explain that longing. It points to something beyond us, and something within us. The inner voice we call our "conscience" may be far more than a habit of upbringing. Many traditions say it is the very presence of the Creator within each person, quietly leading.

If that is true, the search for meaning isn't a problem to fix. It is evidence of what you are.

The thread through every faith

Across the world's traditions, two teachings appear again and again: a portion of the Creator lives within each of us, and our purpose is to become one with it.

Christianity“The kingdom of God is within you.”Luke 17:21, King James Version
Judaism“The spirit of man is the candle of the Lord, searching all the inward parts.”Proverbs 20:27, King James Version
Islam“We are closer to him than his jugular vein.”Qur’an 50:16
Hinduism“Tat tvam asi”: that thou art. The self within is one with the infinite.Chandogya Upanishad 6.8.7
BuddhismAll living beings have Buddha-nature: the seed of awakening is already within.Mahayana Mahaparinirvana Sutra
The Society of Friends“Walk cheerfully over the world, answering that of God in every one.”George Fox, 1656

Jesus prayed “that they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee” (John 17:21). The Urantia Papers call the presence within the Thought Adjuster. The names differ; the thread is the same.
